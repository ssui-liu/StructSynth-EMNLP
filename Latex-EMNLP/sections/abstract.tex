%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Tabular data derives its value from inter-feature dependencies, yet preserving them during synthesis is fragile when samples are scarce. Existing approaches either learn dependencies implicitly through distribution fitting, rely on statistical graph learning that becomes unstable with few samples, or encode structure through flat text serialization. Recent graph-aware methods use dependency graphs as attention masks or distribution priors, but none treats the graph as a generation plan that organizes the LLM's generation process. We introduce \textsf{StructSynth}, a framework that treats a dependency graph as a \textit{generation plan}---determining the generation order, conditioning context, and scope of each black-box LLM call. In \textbf{Evidence-Grounded Graph Induction}, LLM reasoning and statistical association cues jointly construct a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) from limited samples. In \textbf{Graph-Planned Conditional Synthesis}, this DAG drives autoregressive synthesis in topological order, conditioning each feature on its parents to enforce dependency adherence by construction. Experiments show that \textsf{StructSynth} achieves state-of-the-art downstream utility and privacy preservation while maintaining competitive statistical fidelity, especially in low-data scenarios.
\end{abstract}
