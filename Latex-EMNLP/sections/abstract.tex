%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Tabular data derives its value from inter-feature dependencies, yet preserving these dependencies during synthesis is particularly fragile when samples are scarce. Existing approaches either learn dependencies implicitly through distribution fitting, rely on purely statistical graph learning that becomes unstable with few samples, or encode structure only through flat text serialization. Recent graph-aware generation methods incorporate dependency graphs as attention masks or distribution priors, but none treats the graph as a generation plan that organizes the LLM's own generation process. We introduce \textsf{StructSynth}, a framework that treats a dependency graph as a \textit{generation plan}---determining the generation order, conditioning context, and scope of each black-box LLM call. In \textbf{Evidence-Grounded Graph Induction}, LLM-based reasoning and statistical association cues provide complementary signals to construct a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) from limited samples. In \textbf{Graph-Planned Conditional Synthesis}, this DAG serves as an executable generation plan: synthesis proceeds autoregressively in topological order, conditioning each feature on its parents to enforce dependency adherence by construction. Extensive experiments demonstrate that \textsf{StructSynth} achieves state-of-the-art downstream utility and privacy preservation while maintaining competitive statistical fidelity, proving especially effective in low-data scenarios.
\end{abstract}
