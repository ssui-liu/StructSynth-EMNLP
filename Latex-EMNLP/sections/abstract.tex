%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Tabular data derives its value from inter-feature dependencies, yet discovering these dependencies is the first point of failure when samples are scarce---and without a reliable structure, no generative model can produce dependency-faithful synthetic data. Existing approaches either couple structure discovery with generation so both collapse under scarcity, rely on purely statistical graph learning that becomes unstable with few samples, or bypass explicit structure entirely. We introduce \textsf{StructSynth}, a \textit{discover-then-synthesize} framework that decouples dependency structure mining from data generation, reducing per-stage sample complexity. In the discovery stage, LLM-based reasoning and statistical association cues provide complementary signals to construct a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) from limited samples. In the synthesis stage, this DAG serves as an executable blueprint: generation proceeds autoregressively in topological order, conditioning each feature on its parents to guarantee structural adherence. Extensive experiments demonstrate that \textsf{StructSynth} achieves state-of-the-art downstream utility and privacy preservation while maintaining competitive structural fidelity, proving especially effective in low-data scenarios.
\end{abstract}
