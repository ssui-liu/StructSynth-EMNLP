\section{Conclusion}

We presented \textsf{StructSynth}, a framework built on one core idea: treating a dependency graph as a generation plan that determines the order, conditioning context, and scope of each black-box LLM call for tabular synthesis. Separating graph induction from data generation allows each stage to leverage complementary signals---LLM semantic priors and statistical association cues for structure discovery, the discovered topology for dependency-faithful synthesis.
Experiments across six real-world datasets show that this generation-plan design achieves state-of-the-art downstream utility and privacy preservation while maintaining competitive statistical fidelity, with consistent gains across diverse LLM backends confirming the generalizability of the approach.
More broadly, our results suggest that \emph{explicit structural guidance} is a promising direction for tabular synthesis under data scarcity: rather than scaling model capacity or training data, investing in a reliable dependency blueprint yields outsized returns in generation quality.
